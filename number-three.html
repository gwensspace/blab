Closets & Ships

Posted on August 15th, 2026


Warning: Long read. A blanky, tea/coffee/drink and snacks are suggested.



"How did/do you know you're gay?"
um.. How do YOU KNOW you're even straight?

Background: family "super religious" when convenient, of course.
Homosexuality= of the devil.
Masturbation= of the devil.
Even MEDITATION= of the devil.
Drugs= of the devil.
Not doing what the mother would say= of the devil.

You know... because of the topic, a TW may be needed.

Apparently, (because I don't remember) I would get in fights with boys in kindergarden. I just remember not liking (and eventually being jealous of) boys because they kept playing with the girls I liked. Clearly at that young age, "liking someone" doesn't look the same as when hormones hit nor when our frontal lobes meet. But the preference was always there.

I'd see couples and think to myself "I want a friend like that" or "I want a girlfriend like that" or "I want a wife like that" and it was always "she's my favorite" In movies, shows, at the mall, it didn't matter- that was always there, even if I didn't fully know/understand what that meant. I'd just notice humans always being in pairs, and when imagining/wishing my life/future I'd always see mine being with another woman.
Of course, eventually, that thought of wanting my wife/girlfriend/etc. turned into that hateful jealousy toward boys and then men altogether, which also turned into deep self-hatred, self-rejection, self-resentment.

I'm not sure whether I was in the first grade, it may have been before, but I remember my mother giving me a talk about how "girls are like the socket, and boys are like the plug. Two sockets or two plugs don't go together. They don't work unless it's a socket and a plug." with some sort of scripture or whatever.

If that doesn't scream "this kid is gay" I'm not sure what does.

Another thing, I used to be able to draw very well, naturally. I could take small pictures of cartoons and draw them bigger. Very impressive for a kid and for someone who never took any sort of art class by that point. Of course, my mother and brother always said things like "it could be better." (stfu. I was 7 and 8 and they looked so similar- I wish I still had them.) So, I stopped drawing. I find it funny how as a kid, I could quickly find the hatred towards unfair criticism, double standards, and misogyny. It took me until my late 20s to draw again and surprisingly I'm still good at it, it just takes me forever because, even though I know good and damn well I haven't practiced in decades+, it makes sense why I'm not going to be drawing fast like the artists you see popping drawings on the fly- in minutes. I still feel insecure about it and impatient, so I do it maybe once or twice a year.

Anyway, before I quit drawing, there were times where I would draw something cool and give it to the girls I had crushes on, mainly because my rebel self felt like "sure, you just said liking girls wasn't okay, but I'm still going to do it, you just won't know about it." Unfortunately, I was never lucky enough to cross paths with girls who liked girls, so their responses were literally "this is not okay. This is the type of thing boys give to girls." At least one girl I vividly remember saying this to me. The rest, I just remember them giving me looks like the look that girl gave me. Don't even ask why I get to remember that one girl and not the others. lmao.

There were other things that influenced the way I acted towards others and myself besides that socket and plug speech.
"Nobody has your best interest at heart but me" "You can't trust anyone" "Don't trust anyone" "Don't tell anyone anything about you/us" "Nobody loves/cares about you like I do" -the mother.
I can't remember the exact words, but essentially she also said something along the lines of me not being able to be friends with most kids because kids end up fucking each other.
(Did I believe THAT? No, that was dumb. My critical thinking skills were non-existent for the most part, but it kept clashing with most of my brain because kids weren't doing that AND there were two kids' houses I felt safe in, so I'd sneak out to go and spend some afternoons with them, while my mother was at work. I learned early to leave the phone's receiver off the phone, so that anytime anyone called, it'd sound like the line was busy, and I'd be gone for a few hours.)
Then, getting molested by a family member 11 years older at one point, family member's friend at another, one of mother's boyfriend at another, mother's friend's sons at other times, all felt like perfect religious punishments, because each happened to happen after each time I'd make my move on girls. Synchronicity.

I truly believed everyone in whole world felt the same as my mother felt and that was the why I thought gay people only existed on TV AND the why things kept happening. So, my very last attempt to find a loophole was to test that dumb thing above my mother said and try to hook up with some boys in my class when I was.. 11 maybe? Right before leaving Venezuela. There were a couple of girls in my class I crushed on so bad. They were bff with these boys, and based on all these messages I kept getting at home, I tried to do the thing kids allegedly did just to get close with these girls, so I tried to hook up with those boys to find my "in" with the girls.

1-thankfully none of us knew what we were doing, there was never "socket and plug" action.
2- OBVIOUSLY, the logic wasn't logic-ing, so my plan to get close to these girls didn't work- it backfired.
3- The second good news was that shortly after that, we moved to the US. So, my reputation of being a "whore" didn't go far.

I, honestly, wish I knew HOW I would come up with half the shit I thought made sense.
I wish I could go back and hug that girl and just show her how to regulate her nervous system, breathe, and tell her not to despair, that it's going to be okay, the people that keep crossing her path may not be her people and it's okay, but just keep breathing because they'll show up soon. Just chill.

Then, once we moved to the U.S. I was able to chameleon it up for a while.
...UNTIL I was able to get my hands on anything that made me black out and at that point.. well, ever heard the saying "you take yourself everywhere you go"? The mix between active addiction, being in the closet, hormones hitting, and having no self-esteem, nor self-respect definitely made my late teen years painful and hard. I never did substances with the intention of having a good time with everyone else, I did them to blackout as soon as possible and hopefully never come back. The longer I did that the worse it got-and fast.
The "do you remember what you did last night?" and "do you remember what happened last night?" texts and calls sucked every time.
And you guessed it right. My first kiss with a woman? First time being intimate with a woman? Nope. Sadly, vaguely remember.

Then, at 20 years old, I decided to start going to twelve step fellowship meetings. I wanted a break from the consequences of using, blacking out, the things I did and the things that kept happening while blacked out. My intention was never to stay for long. For a fun time, not a long time. But I went to meetings every day, got a sponsor, did service, did stepwork, made friendships with people doing those things, and from the first day, I felt something I had never felt before. I felt love, care... when people hugged me, I didn't feel like there was an expectation from me. I slowly started to feel some sort of self-esteem and self-respect, and pretending I was into men just felt SO excruciating. When I had about 30 days clean, I told the people I hung out with the most that I was "bi" just to test the waters. Even though it was clear that people didn't feel the same as my mother, I still had doubts, but I wanted to see what the response would be sneaking in that bare minimum- you know, yeah.. dudes, but also girls, maybe. lol. But the struggle was real, so somewhere between 40 and 50 days clean, I hooked up with some dude I knew, and... yikes. I couldn't black out this time because I was clean, so I did this sober AF. Oooof. By the time I had 60 days clean, I couldn't even pretend and that's when the words came out of my mouth for the first time. "I am a lesbian. I like women. I don't like men and I never have. "

Then, I met my soulmate, she and I got married and lived happily ever after. The end.

Lmao I wish. Unfortunately, jk.

Being out of the closet just meant learning to navigate life outside of the male-white supremacist-misogynistic-gaze. Did I know that's what I needed to learn back then? Absolutely tf not.
Look, I had the expectation that now that I was out of the closet, women were going to start just falling on my lap, I was soon going to meet my wife, and grow old with her. 20 years old, off substances, self-esteem and self-respect were slowly restoring, I could stand a little straighter, look people in the eye a little longer... But the women I kept being interested in weren't... emotional available, into women, or out of the closet. I just kept looking at the same type of women I was used to encountering my whole life. Same person in every person. It was all about what they looked like, what they wore, who they hung out with, what they did, what they had, straight presenting...

Aside from all that, working through internalized homophobia every time I liked a woman after coming out of the closet slowly got better, obviously.. for the most part. "Habits die hard" am I right. Ha. I was about 23-ish when I started noticing a familiar feeling anytime I was attracted to specific types of women. It took me a minute to put two and two together. With some women, it felt like "whatever" being into them.. others, not so much. Then it finally dawned on me. It was their skin color, race, nationality. That shit was crazy, because imagine being attracted to someone who looks like you or is darker than you and feeling some type of way about it.
So, you see, what had happened was.. apparently, my family is racist af. That was the environment I was raised in. Diplomatic towards people with melanin in the skin, but racist nonetheless. I kind of find it hard to talk about that only because there aren't any specific quotes that were said I can share, except for the, you know, occasional "N" word. Everything else I have is subtle and plausible, and they will never say out loud they're racists because "they have black friends", meanwhile the black friend or two they DO have are those "Roaches for Raid" type of people, as other non-white friends they may or may not have-- You know what, I had about a 600 word count list worth of exhibits supporting the claim/behaviour, but my entries aren't about them. They can get their own blog. LMAO.

Okay, SO, I finally got to figure out I had some deep internalized racism and that was going to take some work to get through (way before I realized anti-racism is constant-neverending work, just like the deconstruction from religion and internalized homophobia), and when that layer of deconstructing started to take place, it just felt like I was coming out of the closet AGAIN.

I wish I could say that was all it took to somehow finally synchronize with finding my forever person or even have the ability to have a successful romantic relationship. Unfortunately, it wasn't. I have come to believe in order to be able to be an actual partner to someone, the relationship with one's self is the catalyst to have any successful relationship- platonic and romantic. After that, the personal relationships. Without the two, my "picker" kept pointing me to the same people (platonically and romantically) since childhood over and over and over again. Even after all the internal work I started in my early 20s, I didn't start talking to ME with love, care, respect, compassion, gentleness and empathy, until a breakup I went through at 24. It was the third one ever. (Technically, second, but have you ever gone through a breakup with someone you never actually dated nor even touched? Lmao. If you have, welcome. Those are just awful and THAT is why, kids, you should leave the closeted/unavailable people alone. LOL.) The free trial only lasted about 90 days, but the emotions were so intense, when it ended, it just felt like air got sucked out of me and there was no way to get it back in, I couldn't sleep, I couldn't eat, couldn't stop crying. It was my first time ever feeling like that and my first time ever treating me as if I was a hurt kid needing care. It was bizarre, but also fascinating witnessing how for the first time in my life, I was giving myself what I kept looking for others (from childhood to adulthood) to give to me. It was surprisingly beautiful.

Maybe it was the age and the frontal lobes starting to connect.. Maybe it was the four years worth of work I had done by that point and the focus on regulating my emotions without substances nor acting out... Maybe it was Maybelline.. Who knows, really. Up until that point my focus was to marry that woman that made my heart skip a beat at the time and secure growing old with them by marrying them, even with my toxicity and all. I really did believe I was the last drop of water that made the last drop of Coca-Cola in the desert back then. Looking back, I feel like those breakups were more about the disappointment of failing to find that forever person in the timeline I had created and having to continue the search for them than anything else.

Since the start of me talking to and treating myself better in excrutiating times, things that were okay before slowly (and one by one) stopped being okay- several things started happening that kept showing me how to set (and keep) boundaries and let go, every time quicker and a little bit easier than the previous times:

- After that last "breakup", I gave myself a year before playing with someone else. Then, when the next person came along and that didn't work out, it wasn't as dramatic as the previous times. I was able to notice I was able to stand my ground without "fear of ending up alone" nor being a dick about it, and it wasn't painful. It was what it was and life carried on. After that, I gave myself an indefinite amount of time that has turned into forever. Which that also has helped me notice red flags early on without having to crochet a full blanket out of them and getting physically intimate early on, before getting emotionally attached/invested.
- Then, around 2017-2018, my dog showed me yelling and cussing wasn't the best approach to express my anger, frustration and overestimulation, even if that's what others do to be heard and listened to and whatnot. It was done to me and I have seen others do it to their kids in my childhood and adulthood, so I thought it was normal. Poor thing, this was in his first year and I haven't yelled at him since, even when he's being a shithead and driving me insane. The living amends I make to him on the daily are forever.
- In 2018, witnessing my mother attempt to get physical (as in fight, chest bumping, etc.) with me for setting up the utilities in my name after finding out she hadn't paid rent in months-on purpose. And being able to move on. I have been happily no-contact since 2019.
- Letting go of "friendships" with people who don't have my best interest at heart. People who don't stick up for me nor have my back, people who are snarky and passive-aggresive towards me, people who don't even like me. Which all was possible by experiencing the friendships who genuinely DO love me, have my best interest at heart, and DO do (lol doodoo) all those things and more.

What is it kids say in the streets nowadays? "We teach others how to treat us" and "what we allow continues."

It's funny how for the longest time I believed that once I came out of the closet, I'd find my person. Then I came out and it felt like being out wasn't worth it until I found my person, which made all that chase and search feel SO necessary and urgent, because how else would they find me? ha.
Yeah... my life is messy, complicated and impossible on the outside (for now), so I guess it works out while it all gets sorted out. The relationships in my life today have been more about quality than quantity- like they were at one point in my life. And for that I will forever be grateful.

I believe that's enough for this novel. What's YOUR coming out story?
